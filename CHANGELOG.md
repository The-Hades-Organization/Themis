# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) (pre-1.0: minor versions may break the API), with a Breaking section for changes that need action when upgrading.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Importers for Casbin and OpenFGA: `strig::import::casbin` and `strig::import::openfga` (feature `import`, no IO) read a Casbin `model.conf` and `policy.csv` (ACL, RBAC with domains, `keyMatch` and ABAC) or an OpenFGA model (DSL or JSON) and its tuples, and return a `Policy` with a `Report` of what was converted, narrowed or not converted. A matcher or condition with no faithful translation is an error with its position and reason, never an approximation that allows more. `strig import casbin|openfga ... -o <dir> [--language ...]` in `strig-cli` writes `policy.strig`; exit `0`/`1`/`2`. Equivalence is tested with fixtures of requests and answers taken from each library's documentation.
- Change impact before apply: `Governance::impact` (and `GovernanceAt::impact`) returns an `Impact` for a `Mutation` without applying it: who gains and who loses access, to which permissions, on which resources and fields, by comparing the decisions over the grants before and after. The subjects and resources come from an explicit sample (`ImpactSource::Sample(SampleSpec)`) or are derived from the reverse queries restricted to the permissions the change touches, including those implied by them (`implied_by`), those that depend on them (`depends_on`) and fields. `QueryLimits` apply and a cut marks the impact `partial` with a note; a decision that could not be settled (a condition or an override that cannot be evaluated) is `indeterminate`, never a gain. `apply_with_impact` applies and returns the impact (`AppliedWithImpact`), the existing `apply` is unchanged.
- Approvals: `Approvals::with_impact` keeps the impact in the `PendingRequest` at `propose`, `approve` recomputes it over the grants of that moment (`Approved::proposed_impact` has the proposed one) and `Approvals::review_impact` returns both and whether they differ, for the approver. Stores keep the request whole, so the Postgres store and the JSON store of the JS binding need no migration and older requests read as without impact.
- Audit: at `detailed` and `forensic`, the record of a change applied with its impact carries the optional field `impact` (`gained`, `lost`, `indeterminate`, `partial`, up to 10 `examples`), documented as field 17b in `docs/audit-format.md`. Records without it keep the canonical form and the hash they had.
- Bindings: `engine.impact(input)` and `engine.reviewImpact(input)` in JavaScript (native and wasm), `impact` in the input of `apply`, `propose` and `resolve` (`result.impact`, `request.impact`, `proposedImpact`), `Approvals` option `impact` and `Approvals.reviewImpact`, with the types `Impact`, `AccessChange`, `ImpactOptions` and `ImpactSource`; `impact` and `review_impact` in `strig-python`, and `Impact`/`ReviewImpact` in the Go and JVM bindings. See Seeing the impact of a change before it applies in the README.
- "Why not?" with a suggestion: `Engine::why_not(ctx, request, &WhyNotOptions)` answers a denial with its cause (`Cause`: an override, the deciding `deny`, no applicable grant, a missing prerequisite, an unknown or `override_only` permission) and the smallest changes that would make it an `allow` (`Remedies`): the condition that failed or could not be evaluated (`SatisfyCondition`, no value invented), an `allow` on the most specific step of the path (`AddGrant`, checked with `validate_grant`, fields included), the deciding `deny` narrowed or removed (`NarrowDeny`, `RemoveDeny`) and a role with an applicable `allow` (`AssignRole`). Each remedy is verified by deciding again on a copy and the ones that do not come out `allow` are dropped; known catalog governance (`management_only`, `non_delegable`, `override_only`) and edits that reach other subjects are marked as `Caveat`s. An override is named and offers no remedy; grants of other subjects are never listed. `QueryLimits::max_decisions` and `max_remedies` bound the work and mark the answer `partial`. Additive: `Engine::explain` is unchanged. JavaScript and Python (`engine.whyNot`, `engine.why_not`, typed in `index.d.ts` and the stub), the Wasm ABI operation `whyNot`, and `strig-inspect explain` after a denial. The window and the browser inspector do not show it yet.
- Audit verifiable by third parties, without changing the record or its hash. `strig::merkle` (feature `audit`) builds an RFC 6962 / RFC 9162 Merkle tree over a stretch of records (`MerkleTree`, leaf hash `SHA-256(0x00 || record line)`, node hash with prefix `0x01`) with inclusion and consistency proofs and pure verifiers. `strig::timestamp` (new feature `timestamp`, off by default, pulls `rsa`, `p256`, `p384` and `sha2` 0.10 only when on) builds the RFC 3161 `TimeStampReq` for a chain head and verifies the `TimeStampResp` (imprint, nonce, policy, CMS signature, certificate chain to roots you supply, time-stamping key usage; fails closed, no revocation check, no IO). `strig::audit_report::ConformanceReport` summarizes a JSON Lines trail (period, records per action and outcome, changes per actor, refusals per invariant, chain breaks, anchors, stamps). `examples/timestamp_http.rs` sends a request through `curl`. Test vectors from RFC 6962 and a real `freetsa.org` response, documented in `docs/audit-format.md`.
- `strig audit report <trail.jsonl> [--json] [--anchor-head <hash>] [--anchor-start <hash>] [--timestamp <resp.tsr>]... [--root <ca.pem>]...`: the conformance report for people or as JSON; exit `0` conforming, `1` not, `2` usage.
- JS binding: `verifyMerkleInclusion`, `verifyMerkleConsistency` and `verifyTimestamp` (native and wasm, no network) with their types in `index.d.ts`. The wasm build links `getrandom` with its `js` feature so the RSA and ECDSA crates build for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`; verification never draws random numbers.
- Policy analysis in the vocabulary of Strig, `strig::dsl::analysis` (pure, no IO). `diff` compares two versions of a policy and lists the decisions that change (subject, permission, resource, `widened` or `narrowed`, and the grant or override responsible on each side with its position), over an explicit fixture or over the universe derived from the catalogs and the grants of both versions; `QueryLimits` apply and a cut marks the diff `partial`. `lint` reports eight findings with a stable code, severity, position and hint: `L001` shadowed grant, `L002` unreachable deny, `L003` permission nobody holds, `L004` unused role, `L005` redundant `implied_by`, `L006`/`L007` condition always false or true, `L008` field never granted; codes can be ignored by code or name.
- `strig diff <before> <after> [--fixture <file>] [--json] [--fail-on allow|any]` (directories or `.strig` files; exit `1` when a change matches, so CI can block a widening of access) and `strig lint <dir> [--json] [--ignore <code>]... [--config strig-lint.json] [--fail-on error|warning|info|never]`. The `strig-test` GitHub Action gains the optional inputs `diff-base` and `diff-fail-on` to diff the policy against a git ref such as the base of the pull request.
- Bindings: `strig.diff(before, after, options?)` and `strig.lint(policy, options?)` in JavaScript (native and wasm), with the types `PolicyDiff`, `PolicyDiffOptions`, `PolicyDiffFixture` and `PolicyLintFinding`, and `diff`/`lint` in the Wasm ABI.

- Temporary elevation and break-glass (milestone 0.10), on top of the approval flow. `ElevationPolicy` and `ElevationRule` declare, per role or permission, the maximum duration, the approver permission, whether a justification is required and whether break-glass is allowed (with a shorter maximum). `Elevations::request`, `approve`, `reject`, `revoke` and `close_review`: the author never approves their own request, the invariants run again at the approval and the impact of `with_impact` stays visible to the approver; an approved elevation becomes an allow grant `elevation:<id>` or a role assignment with `valid_until` = approval + duration, never past the maximum; a policy that tightened refuses the approval. Break-glass is in force at once, marked, and needs an auditor at `forensic`: its record is written under `FailMode::Closed` whatever the call or the auditor say, so a failing sink denies the elevation, and it leaves a review `pending` that another actor closes (`AuditAction::ElevationReview`, `elevation_review`). Early revocation by the actor or an approver; renewal is a new request. `PendingRequest::elevation` (`ElevationInfo`) and `PendingStore::annotate` (default refuses; the memory and Postgres stores implement it, no migration). Revocation is judged by the invariants as the one who revokes, and a request the policy does not list is refused as `not_in_policy` and audited at the auditor's level. See Temporary elevation and break-glass in the README.
- `strig-sync`: `Hub::elevation_approved` publishes the `Change` of an elevation and schedules its expiry notice at `valid_until`, as for the windows of the `CapabilityCache`; `Hub::elevation_revoked` publishes the revocation; `elevation_change` builds the `Change`.
- Bindings: `Elevations` in `@the-hades-organization/strig/approvals` and `engine.elevate(input)` (native and wasm) with the types `ElevationRule`, `ElevationRequest`, `ElevationTarget`, `ElevationInfo`, `ElevateInput` and `ElevateResult`; `PendingStore.annotate` (implemented by `MemoryPendingStore` and the JSON store); `engine.elevate` in `strig-python` and the Wasm ABI operation `elevate`.
- Access recertification campaigns (milestone 0.10), on top of the approvals, validity windows, impact, elevation and separation of duties. `Recertifications::open` makes a campaign with an item per allow grant and role assignment inside a `RecertScope`, each with its reviewer (`ReviewerRule`: a permission on a resource, a role or a subject, with `ReviewerRoute`s), a deadline, the period a kept item stays certified and a default action for what nobody reviews (`DefaultAction`: `Expire`, `Flag`, `Escalate`). `decide` keeps (certifies for the period), revokes (a `Mutation` through the governance as the reviewer, so invariants and audit apply) or delegates; the reviewer needs the declared permission, never reviews their own access and is refused when separation of duties flags a clash. `close` runs the default action from the deadline on, and `Campaign::report()` counts by outcome and reviewer. `RecertStore` with `MemoryRecertStore` and optimistic `revision`; `PgRecertStore` in `strig-postgres` with an additive migration 5 (`strig_recert`, `PgTables::with_prefix` names `<prefix>_recert`). Audit: actions `recert_open`, `recert_decide`, `recert_close` and the optional field `recert` (field 11c in `docs/audit-format.md`); records without it keep their canonical form and hash. See Access recertification in the README.
- Bindings: `Recertifications`, `MemoryRecertStore` and `RecertStoreError` in `@the-hades-organization/strig/approvals`, `JsonFileStore.recertStore` (the file gains an optional `recert` array, version unchanged) and `engine.recertify(input)` (native and wasm) with the types `CampaignSpec`, `Campaign`, `RecertDecision`, `RecertifyInput` and `RecertifyResult`; `engine.recertify` in `strig-python` and the Wasm ABI operation `recertify`.
- Separation of duties (milestone 0.10). The catalog declares `mutually_exclusive` sets of roles or permissions (`MutualExclusion`, global or per anchor) and `conflicting_actions` rules (`ActionConflict`: whoever did `first` on a resource does not do `then` on it, optionally with `distinct_roles`). The `SeparationOfDuties` invariant refuses the change that would create an accumulation (direct role, inheritance through `RoleGraph`, grant or temporary elevation) with a `Violation` that names the set and both sides; removing and revoking never create one. The dynamic rules are evaluated in every decision before the overrides, with no IO, from a history the caller puts in a resource attribute (`history` by default) or in `env`; the denial reads `Reason::Override` named `separation:<rule>`, and a history that cannot be read denies. `Approvals::with_distinct_roles` also refuses an approver who shares a role with the author. `Impact::separation` lists the clashes a change would create (`PotentialClash`), `strig lint` reports sets already violated (`L009`, `separation-violated`) and the policy language gains `mutually_exclusive`, `conflicting_actions` and the `separation_of_duties` invariant in Blocks, Rules and Actors. A policy that uses them exports `"schema": 5` (`DSL_SCHEMA` is 5). The JS types gain `MutuallyExclusive`, `ConflictingActions`, `PotentialClash` and the `separation_of_duties` invariant. See Separation of duties in the README.

### Fixed

- The Python sdist and wheels did not build in the release: `maturin-action` received `pyproject.toml` as `--manifest-path`. The release now passes `crates/strig-python/Cargo.toml`, and a new `python-build` workflow builds the sdist and a Linux wheel with the same arguments on pull requests that touch `crates/strig-python/**`, the workspace manifests or either workflow (also runnable by hand).

### Breaking

- Rust only. `AuditAction` is now `#[non_exhaustive]` (it gained `ElevationReview`, and a variant added later is no longer a breaking change). What to do: add a `_ =>` arm to every exhaustive `match` on it, outside the `strig` crate; the new `ElevationReview` is only written by `Elevations::close_review`, and in the JSON it is `elevation_review` (add it to a closed list of `action` values in a log consumer). Reading, serialising and comparing the values is unchanged. `PendingStore` also has a new method, `annotate`, with a default that refuses, so existing implementations still compile.
- Rust only. `CatalogExport` gains `mutually_exclusive` and `conflicting_actions` and is now `#[non_exhaustive]`, so that a field added later is no longer a breaking change. What to do: build it with `CatalogExport { .., ..CatalogExport::default() }` or through `Catalog::export`, and put a `..` in every pattern that destructures it. `Impact` gains `separation`. JSON and the bindings are unaffected: both fields are optional on read and omitted when empty.
- Rust only. `PendingRequest`, `Approved` and `AuditRecord` gain a public `impact` field (`proposed_impact` for `Approved`) and are now `#[non_exhaustive]`, so that a field added later is no longer a breaking change. What to do: build a `PendingRequest` with `PendingRequest::new(..)` and its builders (`expiring_at`, `with_status`, `with_impact`, `with_elevation`) instead of a struct literal; `Approved` and `AuditRecord` come from `Approvals` and the auditor, so only code that built them by hand changes; and add `..` to any pattern that destructures them. Reading and serialising the fields is unchanged, and stored requests and records without `impact` still load.


## [0.9.0] - 2026-11-28

### Added

- Editor support: the `strig-lsp` crate (a language server over stdio, `lsp-server`, not on crates.io yet) and the VS Code extension in `editors/vscode`. Diagnostics while typing for a whole project directory (several files, any syntax, and `*.strig-test.json`), format document, completion of permission ids, kinds, roles, subjects and condition operators, hover with the definition of a permission, go to definition across files, and a code action that converts the file to another syntax. The extension adds a TextMate grammar for the three syntaxes, a language configuration, the command "Strig: Run policy tests" (`strig test`) and settings for the binary paths. `strig::dsl::ids` is now public so a tool can look an element up in `Origins`. CI builds the crate and lints and packages the extension (`vsce package`, not published). See Editor in the README.
- Inspector in the browser: `playground/inspector.html` (published beside the playground) runs the three tabs of `strig-inspect` over the WebAssembly engine. It loads the scenario JSON of `strig-inspect` from a file, a paste or the built-in example, and shows the decision with its path, roles, overrides and each grant (Evaluation), the permission by subject matrix on a resource (Matrix), and a JSON Lines trail with the `forensic` chain verified and `detailed` and `forensic` decisions re-evaluated against the current policy (Audit). Grants can be switched off or added to simulate, with the original decision shown beside a changed one. Same identity as the playground, light and dark, readable at 360 px, nothing leaves the browser. The playground build copies the example scenario and trail from `crates/strig-inspect/examples/`; the inspector logic (`inspector-core.ts`) has its own tests in `npm test --prefix playground`.
- Envoy External Authorization in `strig-server`: the `envoy` feature (off by default; `tonic` and `prost` only with it) adds `DecisionServer::envoy_service(EnvoyConfig)`, the `envoy.service.auth.v3.Authorization/Check` gRPC service over the same source, clock, limits and work slots as the HTTP routes, and `EnvoyService::serve`. `EnvoyConfig` maps a `CheckRequest` declaratively: the subject from a header or filter metadata (such as `jwt_authn` claims), roles from a header, a route table of methods and path patterns to `RouteRule::public()` or a permission on a `ResourceTemplate` over the path parameters, and an optional `PathResolver`. Allow is `OK`; deny, a route with no rule, no subject, a source that is down, a deadline or a panic are `PERMISSION_DENIED` with HTTP 403 and an optional fixed `x-strig-reason`; paths with dot segments or encoded separators match nothing. With `tracing`, a `strig.server.envoy.check` span and `strig_server_envoy_checks_total{outcome, reason}`. `examples/envoy.yaml` and `examples/envoy.rs`; see `crates/strig-server/API.md`.
- Policy tests: `*.strig-test.json` files beside a policy hold `check` cases (subject, permission, resource, optional `env` and `now`, expected `allow`/`deny`, optionally the reason and the deciding grant) and `mutation` cases (actor and change, `accepted`/`refused`, optionally the refusing invariant), over the grants, tuples and subjects of the policy plus an optional fixture. The runner is `strig::dsl::testing` (`TestFile`, `run`, `Report`; feature `dsl`, no IO) and `dsl::fs::read_tests` reads a directory, with fixtures kept inside it. A case that cannot be judged is `errored`, never a pass. See Testing a policy in `docs/dsl.md`.
- `strig-cli`: the `strig` command, with `strig test <dir> [--junit <file>]` (human report with the diff and the grants considered, JUnit XML, exit `0`/`1`/`2`), and the composite GitHub Action `.github/actions/strig-test`.
- JS binding: `strig.runTests(policy, tests)` runs the same runner natively and on wasm; types `PolicyTestFile`, `PolicyTestCase` and `PolicyTestReport` in `index.d.ts`.
- ORM adapters for filtered listings: `@the-hades-organization/strig/prisma`, `/drizzle` and `/kysely` turn the `expr` of `engine.list(...)` into the `where` of Prisma Client, drizzle-orm and Kysely through a declarative mapping of the resource's anchors and attributes to columns, with the semantics of `PgTables` (allow by kind, anchor and exact id; denies that cut without dropping rows whose column is `NULL`; a residue stays in `residual` with `where` as the upper bound and `settled` as the lower). A kind, attribute or node the mapping cannot express throws `OrmFilterError`, never "all rows". Drizzle and Kysely are optional peers; Prisma needs none. Each module also exports an `OrmAdapter` typed over the `PermissionId`. See Filtering a listing in Prisma, Drizzle and Kysely in the README.
- Field permissions: a grant can name the fields it covers (`Grant::fields`, `fields` in the three syntaxes of the policy language) and a `PermissionDef` can declare the fields a grant may be restricted to (`PermissionDef::fields`). A grant without fields covers the whole resource, and a policy without fields decides exactly as before. `Request::field` asks about one field and is decided by the same strategy over the grants that cover it; `Engine::allowed_fields` answers with a `FieldSet` (`All`, `AllExcept`, `Only`, `None`), `Engine::explain_fields` names the grants that gave and took away each field, and `Explanation` of a request with a field marks the grants that do not cover it (`Verdict::FieldNotCovered`). A field-restricted `allow` never opens the whole resource and a field-restricted `deny` blocks it; a condition that cannot be evaluated never opens a field. `implied_by` carries the fields and `depends_on` is judged on the same field. The validator and `validate_grant` refuse a field outside the declared ones (`GrantError::UnknownField`, `InvalidField`), `NoEscalation` refuses to hand out a field the actor does not hold, and `CatalogConformance` validates the fields. A policy that uses fields exports `"schema": 4` (`DSL_SCHEMA` is 4). See Field permissions in the README.
- JS binding: `engine.allowedFields(ctx, request)`, `engine.explainFields(ctx, request)`, `field` in `DecisionRequest` and `Explanation`, the types `FieldSet`, `FieldVerdict` and `FieldExplanation`, `Grant.fields` and `PolicyPermission.fields`, and `pickAllowed(object, fields)` to mask a response. `PermissionClient.allowedFields(permission, resource)` and a `field` argument on `decide` and `can` decide locally. Capabilities with field-restricted grants carry `withFields` next to a `grants` that older readers decide safely, so an older client fails closed; a set without fields is written as before.
- Python binding: the `strig-python` crate and the PyPI package `strig-auth` (`import strig`, Python 3.9+, one `abi3` wheel per platform, type stubs and `py.typed`) put the same engine behind `Engine`, `Context` and `Exclusive` with the same JSON `EngineSpec`, requests and results as the JavaScript binding: `decide`, `check`, `check_many`, `explain`, `list`, `who_can`, `who_can_on`, `access_of`, `check_for`, `capabilities`, `apply`, `propose`/`resolve`/`expire`, audit (levels, fail mode, sink, redaction, retry queue, `verify_audit_chain`) and the policy language (`strig.dsl`, including `run_tests`). Errors are `StrigError` exceptions whose `code` is the JavaScript `errorCode`; synchronous Python callables serve the `call` condition, overrides, invariants and the audit sink with the JavaScript semantics (an exception denies, no re-entry). The release builds the wheels and the sdist on every tag and publishes them to PyPI through Trusted Publishing once the repository variable `PYPI_PUBLISH` is `true`. `strig-node` gains a `dsl` feature so another binding can reuse its policy-language and exclusive modules. See Python in the README.
- Go and JVM bindings over the same engine, with no native code per language: the new `strig-wasi` crate builds the engine to a plain WebAssembly module (no `wasm-bindgen`, no imports; `strig_alloc`, `strig_free` and `strig_call` with JSON in and a `{ok, value | code, message, diagnostics}` envelope out, the same `EngineSpec` and the same `errorCode`s). `bindings/go` (`github.com/The-Hades-Organization/Strig/bindings/go`, wazero, the module embedded with `go:embed`) and `bindings/jvm` (`io.github.the-hades-organization:strig`, Java 17+, Chicory, usable from Kotlin) expose `Engine`, `Context`, `check`, `decide`, `explain`, `checkMany`, `list`, `whoCan`, `apply`/`propose`/`resolve`/`expire` and the policy language (`compile`, `format`); both are safe for concurrent use, fork an independent instance per worker and fail closed (an error is never an allow, a trapped instance is discarded). Callbacks, the audit sink and exclusive resources are not in this ABI yet. CI builds the module and runs `go test` and the JUnit tests; the release attaches `strig-<version>.wasm`, Go is published by the repository tag, and publishing to Maven Central stays off until the project's account and key exist. See Go and Java and Kotlin in the README.

### Breaking

- Rust only. `Grant`, `PermissionDef`, `CatalogError`, `GrantError` and `Verdict` are now `#[non_exhaustive]`, and `Request` too, so that a field or a variant added later is no longer a breaking change. `Grant` and `PermissionDef` gain `fields`, `Request` gains `field`, and `CatalogError`, `GrantError` and `Verdict` gain variants. What to do: build values with the constructors and builders (`Grant::allow`/`Grant::deny`/`Grant::new` and its chained methods, `PermissionDef::new` and its builders, `Request::new` with `.field(..)`) instead of struct literals, and add a `_` arm to every `match` on those enums. Nothing changes for a policy that does not use fields.

## [0.8.0] - 2026-11-28

### Added

- Postgres storage in `strig-postgres`: `PgStore` loads the grants and the ReBAC tuples into a snapshot that serves the synchronous `GrantSource`/`GrantIndex` (`PgGrants`) and `TupleStore`/`TupleIndex` (`PgTuples`), and reloads on a `Change` through `PgStore::follow` (a failed reload keeps the previous snapshot and shows in `is_stale()`). `PgTables` gains the writes (`delete_grant`, `delete_tuple`, `all_grants`, `all_tuples`), role assignments per subject (`assign_role`, `revoke_role`, `roles_of`, `subject`), pending requests (`insert_pending`, `update_pending`, `due_pending`, ...) and `commit_mutation`, which writes what a governed `Mutation` changes and sends its `NOTIFY` in the caller's transaction (`lock_governance` serialises judge-then-write). `PgPendingStore` is a `PendingStore` with expiry over its own connection. `migrate`/`migrate_with` embed versioned, idempotent, additive SQL (`<prefix>_migrations`); `PgTables::in_schema` and `with_prefix` configure schema and table names. `PgQueryError` gains `Invalid` and `Notify`.
- Observability, opt-in: a `tracing` feature (off by default, zero cost off) in `strig`, `strig-sync`, `strig-postgres`, `strig-redis` and `strig-server` adds spans through `tracing` and metrics through the `metrics` facade. Spans cover `decide`, `check` and `explain`, `list` and `who_can`, `Governance::apply`, the approval flow, each audit write, the hub, the relay and the sessions, the Postgres and Redis transports, and the decision server and its client. Metrics cover decision latency and decisions by effect and reason, applied and refused changes by invariant, the audit retry queue and its drops, open sessions, transport reconnections and the client circuit breaker. Labels never carry a subject, a resource or an attribute; a test in each crate enforces it. The core still has no IO and still compiles to WebAssembly with and without the feature. `crates/strig-server/examples/otlp.rs` exports both over OTLP. The JavaScript binding is not instrumented yet. See Observability in the README.
- JS binding: `engine.checkMany(ctx, requests)` and `engine.explainMany(ctx, requests)` decide a batch in one crossing of the boundary against one `Context` and one instant.
- Exclusive resources across processes: `PgExclusiveStore` (`strig-postgres`, migration v4 adds `<prefix>_exclusive`, one transaction with a row lock per decision, a connection on a thread of its own with a fail-closed timeout) and `RedisExclusiveStore` (`strig-redis`, `WATCH`/`MULTI`/`EXEC`, the caller's clock decides and the native expiry only reclaims after a retention) are `ExclusiveStore`s for `ExclusiveRegistry`. The trait gains `acquire_lease`, `renew_lease`, `release_lease` and `remove_expired` with defaults over `get`/`put`/`remove`, so stores that already exist keep working; `Acquisition::decide`, `OwnedChange`, `Lease::granted` and `Lease::renewed` carry the one rule every store applies, and `strig::exclusive_conformance::run` (feature `conformance`, not default, not stable API) is the battery every store passes. Both crates have an `exclusive` feature, on by default. With `tracing` on, the exclusive stores add one span per decision (`strig.postgres.exclusive.*`, `strig.redis.exclusive.*`), without identities.
- React and Angular bindings: `@the-hades-organization/strig/react` (`StrigProvider`, `useCan`, `<Can>`, `useStrigStatus`; React 18 and 19, SSR-safe) and `@the-hades-organization/strig/angular` (`provideStrig`, `StrigService.can()` as a `Signal<boolean>`, the standalone `*strigCan` directive and the functional guard `strigCanActivate`; Angular 17+), both over the real-time `PermissionClient`, generic over the `PermissionId` from `strig-types`, and denying without a connection, without capabilities, during a resync and on the server. React and Angular are optional peers imported statically. See React and Angular in the README.
- Continuous fuzzing: `fuzz/` (cargo-fuzz, outside the default workspace) with targets for the policy language (`parse`, `format` idempotence, `convert` round trip between the three syntaxes), `EngineSpec` from JSON, condition evaluation (`ip_in`, `time_between` with `tz`), decisions over arbitrary grants (never an allow without a candidate), the `strig-sync` and `strig-server` messages and audit chain verification, seeded from the DSL examples with a DSL dictionary. The `fuzz` workflow runs a short round on pull requests and a long one nightly, keeping any crash as an artifact.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-11-26

### Added

- Pending requests for a change (`Mutation`): `ApprovalPolicy` names, per kind of change (`add`, `remove`, `assign_role`, `revoke_role`), the permission an approver must hold and on which resource. `Approvals::propose` judges the change with the invariants and, when the policy asks for approval, keeps it as a `PendingRequest` (id, author, mutation, instant, optional expiry) instead of applying it; a kind with no rule applies at once, as before. Another actor approves or refuses it (`approve`, `reject` with a reason): the author never decides their own request, the approver needs the permission, and an approval runs the invariants again over the current grants, as the author, before applying; if they now refuse, the request stays pending. `expire_due` closes the requests whose expiry came. Every step is audited (`mutation_propose`, `mutation_approve`, `mutation_reject`, `mutation_expire`, outcome `pending` for a kept proposal, `PendingRef` in the record) under the fail mode of the call. Requests live behind the `PendingStore` trait (`MemoryPendingStore` in memory). In JS: `engine.propose`, `engine.resolve`, `engine.expire` and `Approvals` with `MemoryPendingStore` from `@the-hades-organization/strig/approvals`; `JsonFileStore` keeps the requests in the same file (`store.pendingStore`, `store.approvePending`) and a file without them reads as before. The new enum variants and the `pending` field are additive: records and files from before read unchanged.
- Catalog as the source of frontend types: `PermissionDef` takes per-locale `labels` and `descriptions` (optional, serialized only when set, not part of the policy language). The npm package ships `strig-types <catalog.json> [-o out.ts]` and `generateTypes` (`/types`), which write the `PermissionId` union, a `PERMISSIONS` metadata constant and label helpers. `Engine` (including `DecisionRequest`) and `PermissionClient` take an optional type parameter: with it (`Engine<PermissionId>`) `check`/`decide`/`explain`/`list`/`whoCan`/`whoCanOn`/`checkFor`/`can` accept only the ids of the union; without it they accept any string, as before (`PermissionArg<P>`).
- Failure mode of the audit: `FailMode::Closed` (default, the change is refused when the sink fails) or `FailMode::Open` (the change goes ahead and the record waits in an in-memory retry queue with a capacity and a backoff, `RetryPolicy`, keeping the order of the hash chain). Chosen per call, per permission or as the auditor's default, in that precedence. `AuditStatus` (`written`, `pending`, `dropped`) says where a record went, `AuditStats` and a drop callback report a full queue, and `GovernanceAt::fail_mode` / `apply_reporting` carry it through governance.
- The audit sink in JS: `new Engine(spec, { auditSink, auditPolicy, auditRedact, onAuditDrop })` on Node, Bun and wasm, with `failMode`, `redact` and `prevHash` in `audit` of `apply` and `applyTemplate`, `auditStatus` in the result, `engine.flushAudit()` and `engine.auditStats()`. A sink that throws is a failure of the sink.
- An interoperable audit: `docs/audit-format.md` fixes the canonical form of a record and the hash of the chain, with a test vector; `mask_attributes` and `Auditor::with_redactor` mask sensitive attributes before the record is hashed and written; `Auditor::chain_head`, `chain_head` and `verify_chain_anchored` (`verifyAuditChain(records, { start, head })` in JS) check a chain against an anchor published outside, which catches records cut off the end.
- Time zones for `time_between` and `weekday_in`: an optional `tz` with an IANA name (`America/Sao_Paulo`) makes both read the wall clock of that zone instead of UTC, daylight saving time included. It is `"tz"` in the JSON spec, `time_between "09:00" "18:00" tz "America/Sao_Paulo"` and `weekday_in ["mon"] tz "Europe/Lisbon"` in the three syntaxes, `Condition::in_tz` in Rust and `tz?: string` in the TypeScript types. Without `tz` nothing changes, and a policy without `tz` exports the same JSON as before. A policy that uses `tz` exports its canonical JSON with `"schema": 3` (`DSL_SCHEMA` is now 3), so a reader of schema 2 refuses it instead of reading the window in UTC; `Policy::from_json` takes documents with or without `schema` and refuses one above 3. An unknown name is a validation error when the policy loads, with position and hint; evaluated without validation it fails and never allows. The trace shows the local time used (`12:30 America/Sao_Paulo (UTC-03:00)`). The hour skipped by a spring-forward jump never holds and the hour repeated by a fall-back holds on both passes. The database comes from the new default feature `tz` of `strig` (`chrono-tz`): it adds about 0.9 MB to the `.wasm` (2.70 MB to 3.58 MB, 657 KB to 785 KB gzipped); without the feature a `tz` is refused at load.
- JavaScript callbacks in the Node, Bun and wasm bindings: `new Engine(spec, { conditions, overrides, invariants })` registers synchronous functions for the `call` condition, your own override (`allowed`/`denied`/`abstained`, with `except`) and your own governance invariant, applied with `{ kind: 'custom', name }` in `apply` and `applyTemplate`. They enter the normal flow (the `explain` trace names the override, the governance refuses with the violation and an optional `suggestion`, the audit records it). A callback that throws denies instead of crashing; a callback that calls the engine or changes a `Context` gets `REENTRANT_CALL`. New error codes `UNKNOWN_INVARIANT` and `REENTRANT_CALL`.
- A central route table for the JS integrations, deny by default: `authorizeRoutes(options)` for Express and Fastify and `StrigRouteGuard` for NestJS map method and route (`/doc/:id`) to a permission and resource, refuse any route the table does not cover and open a route only with `public: true`. In NestJS a `@Can` on the handler adds a check on top of the table and never opens what the table denies. The new `/testing` subpath (`expressRoutes`, `nestRoutes`, `collectFastifyRoutes`, `auditRoutes`, `assertRoutesCovered`) lists routes with no rule and rules with no route so the host's tests fail when they drift.
- A JSON file store for the JS package, for systems with neither Postgres nor Redis: `@the-hades-organization/strig/store` (`openJsonStore`, `JsonFileStore`) keeps the grants and the role assignments in a versioned file, serves them as the `loadGrants` of `engineProvider`, and persists what the governance accepts through `store.apply(engine, input)`. - Project policy and guides: [docs/versioning.md](docs/versioning.md) (what is public API, what breaks it, semver before and after 1.0, MSRV, supported Node and Bun, and the deprecation policy), [docs/migration.md](docs/migration.md) (before, after and the step for each Breaking entry), a revised [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) (supported versions, private reporting, response times, scope, and how to verify provenance and the SBOM of a release) and the release and deprecation flow in CONTRIBUTING.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-23

### Added

- Policy language (`themis`, `dsl` feature, off by default; builds for WebAssembly). A policy can be written as text in three syntaxes, Blocks, Rules and Actors, that parse to the same `Policy`. `parse`, `print` and `convert` work on text, and `Project` joins several sources into one policy. Errors carry `file:line:column`, a message and a hint. The guide is in `docs/dsl.md`.
- `themis` (`dsl-fs` feature, not for WebAssembly): `read_file`, `read_dir` (into a `Project`) and `write_file`. `read_dir` refuses a policy file that is a symbolic link, with an error naming it.
- `themis-macros`: new crate with `policy!("...")` and `include_policy!("path.themis")`; a policy that does not validate stops the build, with the message and hint at its position. It is a separate crate, not a `themis-auth` feature; see `docs/dsl.md`.
- Exclusive resources (`themis`, `exclusive` feature, off by default). One holder per resource, with priority, preemption and a time limit, through `ExclusiveRegistry` over an `ExclusiveStore`; `acquire_checked` and `renew_checked` ask the engine for the `exclusive.hold` and `exclusive.preempt` permissions. With `audit` on, every change of holder is recorded through `Auditor::exclusive`.
- Catalog governance (`themis`):
  - `PermissionDef::depends_on`: an `allow` of X stands only when every prerequisite is also allowed on the same resource, otherwise the decision is a deny with `Reason::MissingPrerequisite`. The catalog build refuses an unknown prerequisite, a cycle and a prerequisite that no grant could reach. The `Prerequisites` invariant refuses a change that would leave a subject with X and without Y.
  - `non_delegable`, `management_only` and `override_only`, exported in `CatalogExport`, with the invariants `NonDelegable` and `ManagementOnly`. A grant of an `override_only` permission is ignored by the decision (`Verdict::OverrideOnly`).
  - `NoEscalation`: an actor grants a permission, or assigns a role, only where they hold everything they hand out, including implied permissions and the grant's condition, with an optional bypass permission.
  - `NoLockout::with_reserve`: a refused change that would lock everyone out carries a `Suggestion` naming the role assignment that fixes it. Nothing is applied, and the audit record keeps it.
  - `RoleTemplate` materializes into ordinary grants, with the template id and version in the change metadata and no live link back. `Materialized::apply` sends each grant through `Governance` and simulates the whole batch first.
  - Every new rule has an `InvariantSpec` variant, so it can come from JSON.
- Validity windows (`themis`). `Grant` and `RoleAssignment` take `valid_from` and `valid_until` (milliseconds since the Unix epoch, the end exclusive); `Context::at(now)` and `Governance::at(now)` make what is outside its window not exist. Without an instant the engine fails closed. `next_boundary` gives the next start or end, for whoever keeps a cache.
- `themis-sync`: the capability cache cuts its lifetime at the next validity boundary and never serves a capability past its `valid_until`, even after a clock step or a suspend. `Hub::schedule_expiry` delivers a `Change::expiry` at the boundary, so affected sessions receive the new capabilities with no write. `EngineProvider::with_clock` replaces the system clock.
- `themis-postgres`: `window_predicate` and `candidate_predicate` build the parameterized SQL filter that mirrors `Context::at`. The grant table gains `effect`, `valid_from` and `valid_until` columns with `CHECK`s; a table created before needs them added.
- Listing and reverse queries (`themis`):
  - `Engine::list(ctx, subject, permission, kind)` returns an `AccessFilter`: what the subject reaches, without enumerating the table (the whole kind with exclusions, a set of ids, everything inside some scopes, or nothing). What only the resource can settle stays in `AccessFilter::residual` to check row by row and is never read as an allow.
  - `Engine::who_can`, `who_can_on`, `access_of` and `check_for`. Results name the kind of each party (`SubjectRef`), and `check_for` loads the subject through `SubjectResolver` and answers with the path that decided (`AccessVia`).
  - `QueryContext` carries the sources, the instant (`QueryContext::at`) and `QueryLimits`; a query over a limit fails with `QueryError` instead of answering.
  - `GrantIndex` and `TupleIndex` serve grants and tuples by permission, kind or subject; `RelationSchema::objects_with` and `subjects_of` answer the reverse ReBAC questions. `Override::reach` and `Override::subjects` and `Strategy::native()` let overrides and strategies say where they decide.
  - `themis-postgres` has a `query` module: `PgTables::schema_sql`, `put_grant`, `put_tuple`, the index queries and `filter_where`, which turns an `AccessFilter` into a parameterized `WHERE`. Table and column names are validated and values are always bound.
  - Benchmark: `cargo bench -p themis-auth --bench listing`.
- `themis-server`: new crate that serves decisions over HTTP (axum) and fails closed. Routes: `POST /v1/check`, `/v1/check/batch`, `/v1/list`, `/v1/who` and `/v1/access`, plus `/health/live` and `/health/ready`. `now` is the server clock. A source that is down, a query over its limits, a deadline that passes or any internal error answers 503 with the stable body `{"error":{"code":"...","message":"..."}}`, never an allow. The contract is in `crates/themis-server/API.md`.
- `themis-server`: Rust client behind the `client` feature (on by default), with a timeout per call, a circuit breaker on an injectable clock and batches in one call. A network failure, timeout, 5xx or open circuit is `ClientDecision::Denied`, never an allow.
- `themis-node`: the 0.4.0 engine API in the Node and WebAssembly bindings: `list`, `whoCan`, `whoCanOn`, `accessOf` and `checkFor`, `validFrom` and `validUntil`, `Context.setNow` and `setLimits`, the new invariants, `materialize` and `applyTemplate`, and `Exclusive`. Errors have a stable `code`, and responses use camelCase field names. `index.d.ts` types all of it. `Exclusive.renew` takes `(engine, context, subject, resource, ttlMs, now?)` and checks the hold permission, so a holder who lost it gets `EXCLUSIVE_DENIED`. A refused scalar argument (wrong type, `NaN`, infinity, out of range) throws `INVALID_ARGUMENT`; `INVALID_INPUT` stays for the shape of an object.
- `themis-node`: the policy language in the Node and WebAssembly bindings: `compile`, `print`, `convert` and `loadProject`, plus `loadProjectDir` on the native module only. The policy is the canonical JSON, and errors carry a stable `DSL_*` code and `diagnostics` with file, line, column, message and hint. The guide is in `docs/dsl.md`.
- `themis-auth` (npm): web integrations as subpaths, `themis-auth/express`, `themis-auth/fastify` (`preHandler` and a plugin) and `themis-auth/nestjs` (`@Can(permission, resolver?)` and `ThemisGuard`). You give functions that read the subject, the permission and the resource from the request; nothing is inferred. Each runs in local mode or against a decision server. A deny answers 403, a failing or unreachable service 503, an unidentified caller 401 and an extractor error 500 with no detail; the statuses are configurable and the request never passes otherwise. Express, Fastify and `@nestjs/common` are optional peer dependencies.
- `themis-auth` (npm): `themis-auth/remote`, a client for the decision server with a timeout per call, a circuit breaker, batches and the API's error codes. A network error, timeout, any status other than 200, an unreadable body or an open circuit is a deny.
- Release: each `v*` tag creates the GitHub release with the notes from this file and attaches the Node modules (Linux x64 and arm64, macOS x64 and arm64, Windows x64), the WebAssembly package and `SHA256SUMS`.

- `themis`: the new query types (`Expr`, `OverrideReach`, `FilterShape`, `FilterResidual`, `AccessVia`, `SubjectRef`, `QueryError`, `QueryContext`, `PgFilter` and the request types) are `#[non_exhaustive]`.

- `themis`: subjects are identified by id alone in `NoSelfManagement`, `NoLockout` and `Proposal::subject_after`. At `forensic`, role-change records carry `before_digest`/`after_digest` of the subject's roles; the audit `object` includes the scope (`member#org:o1@u1`) and an anonymous subject is recorded as `<kind>:<anonymous>`.
- npm package `themis-auth` (was `@the-hades-organization/themis`, never published), with native binaries for Linux, macOS and Windows plus the WebAssembly fallback. Imports use `themis-auth`, `themis-auth/client`, `themis-auth/server` and `themis-auth/protocol`.
- crates.io: `themis-auth`, `themis-sync`, `themis-redis`, `themis-postgres`, `themis-axum`.

### Changed

- `themis`: the crate is published as `themis-auth`. Depend on it with `themis = { package = "themis-auth", version = "0.3" }`; the library keeps the name `themis`, so `use themis::...` does not change. The directory stays `crates/themis`.
- `themis`: `Mutation` has two new variants. A custom `Invariant` that matches `Mutation` exhaustively must handle `AssignRole` and `RevokeRole`; `Proposal::grant` returns `None` for them. `Auditor::grant_mutation` records a role change as `role_assign`/`role_revoke`.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-21

### Added

- `themis`: permission catalog with validation, decision engine combining RBAC, ABAC and ReBAC, strategies (`MostSpecific`, `DenyOverrides`, `PermitOverrides`, `FirstApplicable`), overrides, `EngineSpec` in JSON/TOML/YAML.
- `themis`: governance of grant changes with invariants, and auditing at four levels (`minimal`, `standard`, `detailed`, `forensic`) with a hash chain at `forensic`.
- `themis`: `Engine::explain` with the full trace of a decision, and audited access decisions (`Auditor::decide`).
- `themis-sync`, `themis-redis`, `themis-postgres`, `themis-axum`: real-time propagation of grant changes over SSE and WebSocket, with Redis and Postgres transports.
- `themis-node`: JavaScript bindings (N-API for Node and Bun, WebAssembly for the browser), real-time client and server in JS.
- `themis-inspect`: inspector with evaluation trace, grant simulation, access matrix and audit trail reading, as terminal commands and an optional gpui window.
